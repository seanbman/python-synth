# MIDI Controllers

Synthepyzer is designed to feel good under a real controller. A keyboard gives you the notes; your playing gives the sound its shape and energy.

## Notes and velocity

Pressing a key sends a note to the synth. Releasing it tells the synth to let the note go. The force of your press can also affect the sound through velocity: a light touch can be gentle, while a harder touch can make the same patch more present.

If notes seem stuck, release the key and check that the controller is still connected. Re-selecting the device can also refresh the connection.

## Pitch bend

The pitch-bend wheel or strip lets you move smoothly above or below a note. It is useful for expressive leads, small corrections in a melody, and slow movements between notes.

Return the wheel to its center before starting a new phrase. The center position is the normal, unbent pitch.

## Modulation

The modulation wheel adds movement to a sound while you play. Depending on the sound, it may make the tone brighter, add vibrato, or bring another layer of motion forward.

Try holding a single note and slowly moving the wheel. This makes it easier to hear what the current sound is using modulation for.

## Sustain

A sustain pedal lets notes continue after you release the keys. This is especially useful for pads, chords, and piano-like sounds where you want your hands free to play the next phrase.

If the pedal behaves backwards, check its polarity setting or reconnect it before opening the instrument.

## Select a controller

Use the controller selector to choose which connected MIDI device should play Synthepyzer. Only the selected device should control the instrument, so you can keep another controller connected for a different task.
