# Playing

Synthesis becomes easier when you make one change at a time. Hold a note, move a control slowly, and listen for the moment the sound crosses from interesting to too much.

## Start with the filter

The filter is the fastest way to make a sound feel darker or brighter. Lower the cutoff to soften the top end. Raise it to bring more brightness and edge back into the sound.

Add a little resonance to emphasize the area around the cutoff. Resonance can give a sound a clear, vocal character, but too much can make it sharp.

## Shape the beginning and ending

The amp envelope controls how a sound moves through time:

- **Attack** controls how quickly the sound arrives.
- **Decay** controls how quickly it moves away from its initial level.
- **Sustain** controls the level held while a key remains pressed.
- **Release** controls how long the sound takes to fade after release.

For a pluck, try a short attack and release. For a pad, make the attack and release longer so the sound grows and fades around your playing.

## Add movement

Modulation gives a steady sound a sense of life. Use a slow modulation rate for gentle movement and a faster rate for a more obvious pulse or wobble. Keep the amount small at first; a little movement can go a long way.

The modulation wheel is a useful performance control because it lets you decide when the movement appears instead of leaving it on all the time.

## Save your favorite changes

When a sound feels right, save it as a preset before experimenting further. Give it a name that describes how it feels or where you might use it, such as “slow glass pad” or “short bright lead.” Synthepyzer stores these sounds in the versioned `.spyzr` format so future features can be added without making today’s patches unusable.
